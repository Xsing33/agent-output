1 · The seven recommendations

Ranked by impact-to-effort. The first two cost nothing but an afternoon.

1

Retire the last two commodity lines

do thislow effortmedium impact

Good news first: your hero is already specific. “Your reps are guessing who to call” is a problem statement, not a category claim, and your metadata tagline is already descriptive. Nothing there needs surgery.

Why: two lines still do the generic work. Your footer tagline reads “GTM Engineering, Not Another Tool” and GTME’s homepage says “Not another SaaS tool. Not another headcount. Not another agency.” The CostGrid card reads “connect the systems that don’t talk”, which is what Vas, Trevor, Golden Ratio and gtm-engineering.io all pitch. Both are fine ideas. Neither is distinctive anymore.

How: swap the footer tagline for a specific outcome line, and move the connection idea lower where it reads as a mechanism rather than a claim. Directions in section 2.

Effort: Under an hour. Two edits, no new pages, no structural change.

2

Put your receipts above the fold

do thislow efforthigh impact

Why: In the entire competitive set, you are the only one with described architectures and specific verifiable numbers. Vas and Founding GTM publish no proof. Trevor Fox has one testimonial. GTME’s testimonials are unnamed and read as invented. Yours is the moat — and right now it takes scrolling to reach.

How: Add a compact proof band immediately under the hero with three numbers: 90.8% of buyer pains went unprobed · 11,000 accounts enriched at 93.9% verified · 9 production systems shipped. Then keep the detail further down.

Effort: Hours. It’s copy and one component.

3

Split the FAQ into spokes — starting with three

do thismedium efforthighest impact

Why: You’ve written roughly fifteen genuinely rankable assets and put them all on one URL. That page can rank for one primary query, and its authority is split fifteen ways. Every one of those questions is a keyword cluster that deserves its own page.

How: Publish /gtm-engineer-vs-revops, /what-is-gtm-engineering and /call-intelligence first. Turn the FAQ into a lean hub that links out. Migration map in section 4.

Effort: Real work — 2–3 days per page done properly. Start with the first.

4

Build the two money pages

do thismedium efforthigh impact

Why: You currently have zero commercial-intent pages. You capture attention on informational queries and then drop it. /gtm-engineer-vs-revops and /gtm-engineering-cost have weak SERPs (small consultancies, recruiters, no dominant player) and sit closest to a buying decision.

How: Build them with the receipt-first pattern, and make sure the RevOps page takes a position rather than summarising. Existing ranking pages conclude “hire RevOps first, always” — you can beat that with an evidence-backed contrarian line.

Effort: 2–3 days each.

5

Add a soft front door next to the diagnostic

considermedium effortmedium impact

Why: Your funnel opens with a paid diagnostic — but Emilio Consulting opens with a free outside-in GTM audit, then a €1,900 blueprint, then the build. Lead Line Partners does something similar: give a company name and work email, receive a researched document, no call required. Both make the first yes trivial. Yours makes it a purchase.

How: Add a free, outside-in read (public signals only — site, search footprint, funnel as a buyer sees it) that ends with “you keep the findings either way.” Keep the paid diagnostic as the next step. You already have the machinery to produce these at volume.

Effort: A page plus a repeatable output. The automation is the interesting part.

6

Add a vertical page — nobody else has one

do latermedium efforthigh impact

Why: All fourteen competitors say “B2B SaaS” and nothing more. You have real proof in logistics/3PL, manufacturing and food & beverage — plus an ICP finding that ~50% of wins came from logistics/3PL when the team assumed manufacturing was bigger. That page has zero competition and cannot be copied by anyone without the data.

How: Vertical-specific proof only: the account model, the buying committee, the signals that matter in that industry. Build it once Tier 1–2 pages are indexing.

Effort: One page, high leverage.

7

Wire the internal links and keep one CTA

do thislow efforthigh impact

Why: Hub-and-spoke only works if the links exist. Orphan pages don’t rank, and the side-links between spokes are the actual “spokes” — not decoration.

How: Rules in section 5. One primary CTA everywhere: the diagnostic. Many entry points, one destination.

Effort: An hour per page, and it multiplies everything else.

2 · Copy fixes: before → after

These are directions, not finished copy — the language you land on should sound like you. But this shows the shape of the fix.

The homepage hero — already correct, leave it alone

Live today“Your reps are guessing who to call.”
Sub: “I build the system that ends the guessing. It picks the account, finds the buyer, writes the message, and hands it to your rep every morning. On your stack, not a new tool.”

This is a problem statement in the reader’s words, and it ends on ownership. It is stronger than anything in the competitive set. Do not change it. An earlier draft of this doc recommended replacing it based on a cached copy of the site. That recommendation was wrong.

The footer tagline

Currently“Xavier Singletary: GTM Engineering, Not Another Tool”

The problem: this is the one line on the site that is close to GTME’s homepage copy. It is also the last thing a visitor reads, which makes it the wrong place to sound like the field.

Direction A“Xavier Singletary: the system that ends the guessing”
Direction B“Xavier Singletary: GTM engineering that runs on your stack”
Direction CKeep the mark in the visual identity, but not in the sentence. Footer reads plain: “Xavier Singletary · xsingletary.com”

The CostGrid card that reads as a claim

Currently“06 · The stack doesn’t talk to itself” — “Whatever the specific gap, the pattern’s the same: extract what’s already there, connect the systems that don’t talk, ship it somewhere your team actually uses it.”

The problem: as a summary card it reads like positioning. It works better as a mechanism, stated after the reader already knows what you build.

SuggestedKeep the card, but lead it with the mechanism and the outcome: “Six problems, one pattern. Every system starts the same way: pull what is already in your tools, score it against what has actually closed, and hand the result to the person who acts on it.”

The proof band (new — goes above the fold)

Suggested 90.8% of buyer pains went unprobed across 318 analyzed calls · 11,000 accounts enriched, 93.9% verified · 9 production systems shipped and running

Three numbers, each specific and each explained further down. Nobody in the set can put a band like this on their homepage.

“I connect your tools” → the architecture

Category default“I connect your tools / connect the systems that don’t talk.”
Suggested“I extract what’s already sitting in your tools, connect it across the systems that don’t talk, and ship it somewhere your team actually uses it — a two-axis Fit + Signal engine that assigns each account a verdict: STRIKE, NURTURE, QUALIFY, or KILL.”

The second version is unfakeable. The first is what everyone says. Describing the mechanism is what separates you from the field.

Ownership lines — say it once, briefly

Emilio, The GTM Lab, Golden Ratio, Growth Engineer and gtm-engineering.io all already promise clean handover. It’s table stakes, not differentiation. Keep your version short and move on — don’t build a section around it.

“AI-native” — stop saying it

Yalc, Growth Engineer, Golden Ratio and Emilio all lead with agents. In 2026 “we use AI” is like “we use email.” Show the AI in the architecture; don’t claim it as a feature.

5 · Internal linking rules

This is the mechanism, not decoration. Orphan pages don’t rank.

  1. Every spoke links up to the pillar (/what-is-gtm-engineering).
  2. Every spoke links sideways to 2–3 siblings. Related problem, natural next question.
  3. Informational spokes link down to a money page. Scoring → vs-RevOps. Call intelligence → cost. This is how attention becomes a lead.
  4. Use descriptive anchor text. “how to replace intent data with in-house signals” — never “click here” or “learn more.”
  5. The pillar links to every spoke. One hub, many doors.
  6. One CTA, everywhere. The diagnostic. No competing asks.
The link graph in one line

Pillar ↔ every spoke · spoke ↔ 2–3 siblings · informational spoke → money page → diagnostic.

6 · Funnel & CTA changes

Keep the diagnostic as the spine — already done

Checked against the codebase: nav, hero, FAQ and footer all pull the same BOOKING_URL from lib/links.ts. There is exactly one CTA and one destination. Nothing to fix here, and nothing should be added. Do not introduce a second primary CTA.

Add one softer entry point

A free outside-in read. It converts colder traffic that isn’t ready to buy a diagnostic, and it gives you a reason to have a conversation. Everyone with a working funnel in your competitive set has a version of this — you don’t.

Answer the price objection on the page

You’re 5–10× the field’s monthly rate. That’s correct for a build you own vs. a service you rent — but it must be said. Growth Engineer ($2–4K/mo, pause anytime) has trained buyers to expect cheap and commitment-free. A dedicated comparison that reframes cost as outcome per dollar and what you keep is the highest-leverage page you’re missing.

7 · What not to do

Each of these is a tempting move that would cost you the one advantage you have.

skip Pages for “AI engineer” and “cold outbound email”

These are synonyms and mechanisms, not audiences. Three synonym pages compete with each other and Google picks one. Worse, presenting as an “AI engineer” or a “cold email guy” plants you in the generic-agency crowd your whole Not Another Tool positioning exists to escape.

Instead: let “AI” live in the architecture and let “cold outbound” be one output channel inside the call-intelligence or outbound story — always with the anti-positioning (“we don’t just send cold email”) that converts serious buyers and repels tire-kickers.

skip Chasing “hire a GTM engineer”

That SERP belongs to competitor homepages and Toptal. You cannot win it with a content page. Use it as competitive intel instead.

skip “Intent data alternative / pricing” shopping queries

Pure purchase intent against real vendors with real budgets (LeadFuze, IntentHub). You’d be bidding against people whose entire business is that keyword.

skip A full site rewrite

You just revamped and the pages are dated 2026-10-08. Another rebuild is SEO churn — you’d discard whatever is currently indexing. Add pages; change copy; don’t restructure.

skip One page per synonym

“GTM engineer” / “GTM engineering” / “AI GTM engineer” as three pages is cannibalization, not coverage. One page, or fold the variants into existing pages.

8 · How to know it’s working

Honest expectations. SEO is slow; these are the leading indicators to watch before revenue moves.

WhenWhat to checkWhat good looks like
Week 1–2Pages indexed in Search ConsoleAll new URLs indexed, no coverage errors
Week 3–6Impressions per pageEach spoke pulling impressions for its target query — impressions, not clicks yet
Week 6–12Position + click-throughMoney pages ranking page 1–2; informational pages feeding traffic to them
OngoingDiagnostic form starts & referral sourceAt least one lead citing organic search
The realistic read

Expect no meaningful organic leads for 60–90 days. That’s normal and not a signal to rebuild. The early signal to act on is impressions — if a page gets impressions but no clicks, your title is wrong, not your content. If a page gets nothing at all after 8 weeks, the intent mapping was off.
